## Why Every Grower Needs a 3-Minute On-Farm Screening Kit

Commercial humic acid is uniquely easy to counterfeit. Because farmers judge liquid concentrates and black shiny flakes primarily by how dark they turn a bucket of irrigation water, unethical blenders frequently substitute or dilute genuine leonardite potassium humate with **pulverized coal dust**, **unextracted raw lignite**, **sulfite paper-mill sodium lignosulfonate**, or **fermented sugarcane molasses**.

While accredited [ISO 19822:2018 laboratory testing](/quality-and-testing/market-malpractices-and-testing) is required for legal certification and bulk B2B procurement, individual farmers and field agronomists do not have a muffle furnace or DAX-8 resin column sitting in their farm shed. Fortunately, three simple physical-chemistry tests—requiring nothing more than **two clear glasses, clean soft drinking water, and ordinary white vinegar**—expose crude adulteration in under three minutes before you invest in a full seasonal supply.

| **Test 2: White Vinegar / Citric Acid (pH ~3)** | **Rapid dark flocculation** — visible brown-black curds settle, clearing liquid above | No change (already insoluble solid dust) | **Resists precipitation** — stays smooth dark reddish-brown | **Zero precipitation** — remains completely dissolved |
| **Test 3: Foam Stability When Shaken** | Minimal, thin bubbles disappearing in **10–15 seconds** | No foam | **Thick, dense soap-like tan foam** lasting several minutes | Light sugary froth |

## Preparation: Choose the Right Test Water First

One common mistake invalidates farm-level solubility checks: **never use raw hard borewell water (&gt;250 ppm CaCO₃) for a purity test**. Ambient divalent calcium (`Ca²⁺`) and magnesium (`Mg²⁺`) inside hard groundwater will naturally flocculate even 100% pure leonardite potassium humate, tricking you into rejecting a clean product. Always run these three tests using **clean bottled drinking water, RO water, or collected rainwater**.

---

## Test 1: Cold-Water Dissolution & Grit Residue Check (60 Seconds)

**Target adulterants caught:** Pulverized bituminous coal dust, raw unextracted lignite ore, high-ash mine tailings, and poorly centrifuged humate that will plug 120-mesh drip irrigation filters.

1. Pour **250 mL of room-temperature clean soft water** into a tall clear glass (Glass A).
2. Scoop **half a level teaspoon (~2 grams)** of potassium humate powder or shiny flakes and drop it gently onto the water surface. **Do not stir for the first 20 seconds.**
3. **Watch the drop pattern:** Genuine spray-dried potassium humate or thin drum-dried flakes immediately release dense, hypnotic ink-black vertical ribbons curling toward the bottom. Coal dust and unextracted lignite repel water initially, sitting as dull hydrophobic grey specks on top before sinking as heavy grit.
4. After 20 seconds, stir slowly for 15 seconds, let stand for 60 seconds, and gently pour 80% of the dark liquid off into a bucket. Tilt the glass toward sunlight and rub your fingertip across the bottom.
   - **Pass:** Virtually clean glass with at most a tiny veil of silky dust (suitable for 120-mesh drip fertigation).
   - **Fail:** Audible gritty sand grains, quartz particles, or heavy muddy black sludge left behind—indicating unextracted lignite, coal fines, or &gt;5% mineral ash.

---

## Test 2: White Vinegar / Citric-Acid Precipitation Check (90 Seconds)

**Target adulterants caught:** Sodium lignosulfonate, caramelized molasses, yeast vinasse, and synthetic black dyes masquerading as Potassium Humate.

1. Pour **200 mL of clean water** into Glass B and add just **1 to 2 tablespoons** of the dissolved solution from Glass A until the liquid looks translucent **light tea-brown** (if the water is opaque jet-black, you won't be able to see flocs forming!).
2. Add **3 tablespoons of ordinary 5% white household vinegar** (or half a teaspoon of dissolved citric acid crystals) and stir gently twice. This drops solution pH to roughly **2.8–3.2**.
3. Place the glass against a bright window or flashlight and observe for **90 to 120 seconds**.
   - **Pass (True Humic Acid):** Protonation neutralizes the carboxylate (`–COO⁻`) groups, collapsing electrostatic repulsion. Within 30–90 seconds, the translucent brown water clouds dramatically, forms visible **dark brown-black curds/snowflakes**, and begins settling toward the bottom, leaving noticeably lighter golden-yellow liquid above.
   - **Fail (Likely Adulterated):** The liquid stays smooth, glossy, reddish-brown, and translucent with little or no dark precipitation even after adding extra vinegar. This is a classic hallmark of paper-mill **sodium lignosulfonate** or sugar dyes.
   - **Crucial Clarification for Fulvic Acid:** True **Fulvic Acid** (golden-yellow to light amber powder) is *chemically supposed to stay 100% clear* in vinegar! If a product sold as "100% Fulvic Acid" drops heavy black curds in vinegar, you were actually sold cheaper potassium humate.

---

## Test 3: Warm-Water Odor, Foam & Tackiness Check (30 Seconds)

**Target adulterants caught:** Subtle partial blends (e.g., 50% potassium humate cut with 50% sodium lignosulfonate or molasses), which can partially pass Test 2 while still carrying hidden pulp-mill or sugar contaminants.

1. Mix **1 teaspoon** of the product into **100 mL of warm water (~45 °C)** inside a small clean bottle with a screw cap.
2. Shake briskly for **5 seconds**, remove the cap immediately, and check three sensory clues:
   - **Foam Layer:** Sodium lignosulfonate is a powerful industrial surfactant used in concrete admixtures and drilling muds. Shaking it builds a **thick, creamy tan foam collar** that persists for several minutes. Authentic leonardite humate forms only fragile bubbles that vanish quickly.
   - **Odor Profile:** Warm water amplifies volatile aromas. Real leonardite humate smells faintly earthy, like wet mineral peat or rain on soil. Lignosulfonate emits a telltale **sweet woody, wet-paper, or faint vanilla (vanillin)** scent; molasses/vinasse smells distinctly of burnt sugar, caramel, or fermented soy sauce.
   - **Fingertip Dry Test:** Rub two drops between thumb and forefinger and let dry for 30 seconds. Molasses-spiked powders feel noticeably tacky/sticky; authentic humate dries completely smooth and matte.

## Closing the Final Gap: Two Flaws Only a COA Can Catch

Even if a product passes all three kitchen tests effortlessly, two serious quality risks remain invisible without laboratory numbers:
1. **Industrial Sodium Humate (`NaOH`):** Extracted with cheap caustic soda instead of potassium hydroxide (`KOH`), sodium humate dissolves cleanly and precipitates identically in vinegar—yet injects 6%–9% clay-dispersing sodium into your irrigated soil.
2. **Trace Heavy Metals &amp; Partial Sulfur Spiking:** Subtle lignosulfonate blending pushes elemental sulfur above **0.75%–2.0% S**, and mined coal seams vary in Cadmium and Arsenic.

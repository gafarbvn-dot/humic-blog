## Two Different Jobs in Clay & Sodic Soil Reclamation

When irrigation water evaporates faster than rainfall drains, or when heavy clay soils lose their organic carbon, fields turn rock-hard when dry, slick and waterlogged when wet, and crusted across the seedbed. Growers facing dispersed clay or salt-affected sodic ground typically hear two competing prescriptions: **"Spread tons of agricultural gypsum"** or **"Inject concentrated potassium humate."**

Soil colloid physics makes the answer unmistakable: **gypsum and potassium humate perform two distinct, sequential chemical tasks—and combining them in the right order reclaims sodic clay significantly faster than either material alone** (Nan et al., 2016).

### What Gypsum (`CaSO₄·2H₂O`) Actually Does
In a **sodic soil** (defined by an Exchangeable Sodium Percentage **ESP &gt; 15%**, or Sodium Adsorption Ratio **SAR > 13**), bloated monovalent sodium ions (`Na⁺`) dominate clay exchange sites and push flat clay platelets apart (**dispersion**). Agricultural gypsum supplies massive quantities of soluble divalent calcium (`Ca²⁺`) without raising soil pH (unlike calcitic lime). Because `Ca²⁺` binds clay surfaces far more tightly than `Na⁺`, calcium physically kicks `Na⁺` off the clay into the soil solution (`Clay–2Na⁺ + CaSO₄ → Clay–Ca²⁺ + Na₂SO₄`), where leaching irrigation water washes soluble sodium sulfate safely below the root zone (Shainberg & Letey, 1984).

### What KOH Potassium Humate Actually Does
Potassium humate does **not** supply bulk calcium to displace tons of exchangeable sodium per hectare. Instead, once calcium brings dispersed clay platelets close together, negatively charged humic macromolecules weave across those calcium bridges (`Clay⁻ – Ca²⁺ – Humate⁻ – Ca²⁺ – Clay⁻`) to lock platelets into **water-stable micro-aggregates** that resist raindrop slaking and surface re-crusting (Piccolo et al., 1997). Simultaneously, potassium humate adds root-zone potassium (`K⁺`) to improve plant `K⁺/Na⁺` selectivity, cushions osmotic salinity shock, and stimulates pioneer root penetration into reclaimed fissures.

## Diagnosis First: Do You Have a Sodic Clay or a Low-Organic Compacted Clay?

Before buying either input, check two numbers on a standard laboratory soil test: **ESP (Exchangeable Sodium Percentage)** and **SOM (Soil Organic Matter)**:

1. **High Sodium (`ESP > 6%` in heavy smectite clay, or `ESP > 15%` in general):** You have a **sodic soil**. Gypsum is chemically non-negotiable. Start with your lab's Gypsum Requirement (`GR`) tonnage, ensure subsurface drainage exists so displaced `Na₂SO₄` can actually exit the root zone, and pair with **KOH potassium humate** to accelerate infiltration and early crop survival. (See our deep-dive on [Sodic, Saline & Sandy Desert Soils](/problem-soils/sodic-saline-and-sandy-desert-soils).)
2. **Low Sodium (`ESP < 3%`), Adequate Native Calcium, Low Organic Matter (`SOM < 1.2%`):** You have a **mechanically compacted, organically depleted clay**—not a sodic soil. Spreading 3 tons/acre of gypsum often disappoints here because sodium isn't the culprit. Instead, fracture plow pans mechanically when dry, then stabilize the open clay crumbs with **KOH potassium humate + compost/cover crops**—detailed in our [Compacted Heavy Clay & Waterlogged Soils Guide](/problem-soils/compacted-heavy-clay-waterlogged-soils).

## The Gold-Standard Combined Reclamation Protocol (Zero Tank-Mix Disasters)

Field reclamation trials (Nan et al., 2016) consistently show that integrating **gypsum + KOH potassium humate** lowers soil ESP, raises saturated hydraulic conductivity (`Ksat`), and boosts crop emergence markedly faster than gypsum alone. Follow this strict operational sequence:

### Step 1: Broadcast & Incorporate Agricultural Gypsum First
Spread soil-test-recommended **agricultural gypsum** (typically 1–3 tons/acre) evenly across the field and disc lightly into the top 10–15 cm before pre-planting leaching irrigation.

### Step 2: Apply KOH Potassium Humate Separately (Granular or Flushed Drip Pulse)
- **Dry Broadcast / Banding:** Apply **4–8 kg/ha (`1.6–3.2 kg/acre`) of granular KOH potassium humate** in the planting row or broadcast separately after gypsum incorporation. In moist soil pores, slow-dissolving `Ca²⁺` from gypsum and humic anions spontaneously cross-link onto clay platelets right where you want them—*in the soil matrix*, not inside your irrigation pipes!
- **Drip Fertigation Warning:** **NEVER mix solution-grade gypsum or calcium nitrate inside the same stock tank as potassium humate.** Instant calcium-humate precipitation will plug filters and emitters. Always screen tank mixes in our [Tank-Mix Compatibility Checker](/tools/tank-mix-checker) and calculate split field rates in our [Dosage Calculator](/tools/dosage-calculator) and [30-Crop Master Dosage Table](/crops/master-dosage-table).

### Step 3: Verify Zero Sodium Contamination on Your Humate COA
Buying unverified bulk humate for sodic soil reclamation is a classic trap: cheap **sodium humate (`NaOH`)** adds 6%–10% sodium right back onto the field you just spent thousands of dollars trying to de-sodify. Audit every batch for `Na < 1.5%` and `ISO 19822` compliance before purchase.

## Bottom Line

**Gypsum is the chemical crowbar that pries sodium off clay; KOH potassium humate is the structural cement that locks the reclaimed clay into breathable, crust-free crumbs.** Never pit them against each other on sodic ground—and never mix them inside the same liquid tank—apply them sequentially in soil to reclaim heavy sodic clay faster and keep it productive long-term.
